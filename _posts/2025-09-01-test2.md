      <h2>Thesis</h2>
      <p>Score matching is useful because it avoids the normalizing constant, but its real value depends on whether estimating local gradients is easier than estimating likelihood ratios for the problem at hand.</p>
    </section>
  </div>

<section class="claim-list" aria-label="Claim summary">
  <article>
    <b>Claim 1</b>
    <p>The method converts density estimation into a gradient-field matching problem, which can be easier for unnormalized models.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <b>Claim 2</b>
    <p>The estimator is sensitive to boundary behavior, so compact-support and heavy-tail examples need careful treatment.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <b>Claim 3</b>
    <p>Modern score-based generative models inherit the same conceptual move, but scale it through time-dependent noise perturbations.</p>
  </article>
</section>
