      <h2>Overview</h2>
      <p>The Wasserstein distance is not just another discrepancy between distributions. It sees the metric structure of the underlying space because it compares measures through transport plans.</p>
    </section>

    <h2>Definition</h2>

    For probability measures \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), define

    <div class="equation">
    $$
    W_p(\mu,\nu) =
    \left(
    \inf_{\gamma \in \Gamma(\mu,\nu)}
    \int d(x,y)^p\,d\gamma(x,y)
    \right)^{1/p}
    $$
    <small>Definition 1</small>
    </div>

    <h2>Proof Sketch</h2>

    The distance is geometric because admissible couplings encode movement across the base space. In the discrete case, a transport plan is a matrix whose row and column sums match the two marginals.
