<section class="abstract">
  <h2>Abstract</h2>
  <p>When samples come from a heavy-tailed distribution, the numerical window is part of the statistical experiment. This note records how truncation choices can change the apparent quality of a fitted score.</p>
</section>

## Observation

The same estimator can look stable on a central interval and unstable on a wider window. This is not just a plotting artifact; it changes the empirical objective.

<div class="equation">
$$
R_T(\theta) = \int_{-T}^{T}
\left\| \widehat{s}_{\theta}(x) - s(x) \right\|^2 w_T(x)\,dx
$$
<small>Windowed risk</small>
</div>

## Interpretation

For very heavy tails, the far window is often governed by sparse samples. A useful diagnostic should therefore report the window, weight, and seed together with the final score error.

<div class="theorem">
  <span class="label">Practical Rule</span>
  Report central accuracy and tail accuracy separately whenever the fitted object is a derivative of a log-density.
</div>
