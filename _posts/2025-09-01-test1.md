    <h2>Motivation</h2>

    Suppose a parametric model changes from \(p_\theta\) to \(p_{\theta+d\theta}\). A natural geometry should measure how distinguishable these neighboring distributions are.

    <div class="theorem">
      <span class="label">Lemma</span>
      The second-order expansion of relative entropy induces a positive semidefinite quadratic form on parameter space.
    </div>

    <h2>Derivation</h2>

    <div class="derivation">
      <ol>
        <li>Start from \(D_{KL}(p_\theta || p_{\theta+d\theta})\).</li>
        <li>Expand the log-density to second order in \(d\theta\).</li>
        <li>Use normalization to remove the first-order term.</li>
        <li>Recognize the Fisher information matrix.</li>
      </ol>
    </div>

    <div class="equation">
    $$
    g_{ij}(\theta) =
    \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta}
    [
    \partial_i \log p_\theta(X)
    \partial_j \log p_\theta(X)
    ]
    $$
    <small>Fisher metric</small>
    </div>

    <h2>Exercise</h2>

    Compute the Fisher metric for a one-dimensional Gaussian model with fixed variance. Then repeat when both mean and variance are free parameters.
