<section class="intro-strip"><h2 id="definition">Max stability</h2><p>What must a copula satisfy to describe componentwise maxima? An [[extreme-value copula]] preserves its form under maxima. Equivalently, for every \(t>0\), \[C(u^t,v^t)=C(u,v)^t.\] <a class="course-citation" href="#ref-1" aria-label="Reference 1">[1]</a> This is the copula analogue of a univariate extreme-value limit law.</p></section>
<div class="math-block theorem"><span class="block-label">Theorem 20.1 <span>Pickands representation</span></span><p>For \(0&lt;u,v&lt;1\), a bivariate extreme-value copula has the form \[C(u,v)=\exp\{(\log u+\log v)A(\log v/(\log u+\log v))\},\] where the Pickands function \(A:[0,1]\to[1/2,1]\) is convex and satisfies \(\max(w,1-w)\le A(w)\le1\) for \(0\le w\le1\). Boundary values are obtained by continuity. Conversely these conditions produce an extreme-value copula. <a class="course-citation" href="#ref-2" aria-label="Reference 2">[2]</a></p></div>
<div class="math-block proof"><span class="block-label">Proof sketch</span><p>Write \(x=-\log u\), \(y=-\log v\), and define the stable-tail function \(\ell(x,y)=-\log C(e^{-x},e^{-y})\). Max stability makes \(\ell\) homogeneous: \(\ell(tx,ty)=t\ell(x,y)\). For \(x+y>0\), homogeneity gives \(\ell(x,y)=(x+y)A(y/(x+y))\), which yields the displayed formula in the interior of the square. The values on the boundary follow by continuous extension. The equivalence between convexity with these bounds and copula validity is the characterization stated in Nelsen, Section 3.3.4. The calculation here derives the homogeneous representation; it does not prove that full characterization.</p></div>
<p>The independence copula has \(A(w)=1\). The comonotone limit has \(A(w)=\max(w,1-w)\). Gumbel has \(A(w)=(w^\theta+(1-w)^\theta)^{1/\theta}\), so its max stability follows directly. The Marshall-Olkin copula supplies an asymmetric extreme-value example.</p>
<details class="supplementary-proof" id="proof-pickands-characterization"><summary>Proof</summary><div class="proof-content"><p>Here is a course derivation of the characterization stated in the cited textbook. <a class="course-citation" href="#ref-2" aria-label="Reference 2">[2]</a> First suppose \(C\) is max stable. It is positive in the open square: if \(C(u,v)=0\) there, max stability would give \(C(u^{1/n},v^{1/n})=0\) for every \(n\), contradicting continuity at \((1,1)\). Thus the stable-tail function \(\ell\) in the proof sketch is finite. The copula margins give \(\ell(x,0)=x\) and \(\ell(0,y)=y\); hence \(A(0)=A(1)=1\). Since \(C(u,v)\le\min(u,v)\), we have \(\ell(x,y)\ge\max(x,y)\). The Fréchet lower bound, applied at \((e^{-x/n},e^{-y/n})\), gives</p><p>\[e^{-\ell(x,y)/n}=C(e^{-x/n},e^{-y/n})\ge e^{-x/n}+e^{-y/n}-1=1-\frac{x+y}{n}+O(n^{-2}).\]</p><p>Here \(O(n^{-2})\) denotes a remainder bounded in absolute value by a constant times \(n^{-2}\), for fixed \(x,y\). Letting \(n\) increase yields \(\ell(x,y)\le x+y\). On \(x+y=1\), these inequalities become the stated Pickands bounds.</p><p>It remains to prove convexity. For \(x_1&lt;x_2\) and \(y_1&lt;y_2\), the copula rectangle inequality applied to \(C(e^{-tx},e^{-ty})=e^{-t\ell(x,y)}\) gives</p><p>\[e^{-t\ell(x_1,y_1)}-e^{-t\ell(x_2,y_1)}-e^{-t\ell(x_1,y_2)}+e^{-t\ell(x_2,y_2)}\ge0.\]</p><p>Its constant terms cancel. Divide by \(t\) and let \(t\downarrow0\) to obtain \(\ell(x_1,y_1)-\ell(x_2,y_1)-\ell(x_1,y_2)+\ell(x_2,y_2)\le0\). Thus \(\ell\) is submodular. If \(\ell\) is smooth, write \(s=x+y\), \(w=y/s\), and \(\ell=sA(w)\). Direct differentiation gives \(\ell_{xy}=-w(1-w)A''(w)/s\). Submodularity says \(\ell_{xy}\le0\), so \(A''\ge0\).</p><p>The same conclusion holds without smoothness. Average \(\ell(e^a x,e^b y)\) over small \(a,b\) using a nonnegative smooth kernel of total mass one. Each function being averaged remains homogeneous and submodular, and the average \(\ell_\varepsilon\) is smooth in the positive quadrant. Its function \(A_\varepsilon(w)=\ell_\varepsilon(1-w,w)\) is convex by the preceding derivative argument. Continuity of \(\ell\) gives \(A_\varepsilon(w)\to A(w)\) for \(0&lt;w&lt;1\); a pointwise limit of convex functions is convex. Endpoint continuity follows from the bounds and copula margins. This averaging uses the general smoothing tools described in the <a href="/courses/copulas/appendix-background/#approximation">background appendix</a>.</p><p>Conversely, suppose \(A\) is convex with the stated bounds. First assume \(A\) is smooth and set \(\ell(x,y)=(x+y)A(y/(x+y))\), \(C(u,v)=e^{-\ell(-\log u,-\log v)}\) in the open square. Differentiation gives \(\ell_x=A-wA'\), \(\ell_y=A+(1-w)A'\), and \(\ell_{xy}=-w(1-w)A''/s\le0\). Convexity and \(A(0)=A(1)=1\) give \((A-1)/w\le A'\le(1-A)/(1-w)\); the lower Pickands bound then implies \(\ell_x,\ell_y\ge0\). Consequently</p><p>\[C_{uv}(u,v)=\frac{C(u,v)}{uv}\bigl(\ell_x\ell_y-\ell_{xy}\bigr)\ge0.\]</p><p>Thus interior rectangles have nonnegative probability. The endpoint values \(A(0)=A(1)=1\) give \(C(u,1)=u\) and \(C(1,v)=v\); the lower bound \(\ell\ge\max(x,y)\) gives zero on the lower edges. Therefore \(C\) is a copula. Homogeneity of \(\ell\) makes it max stable.</p><p>For a general convex \(A\), use its Bernstein polynomials \(A_n(w)=\sum_{k=0}^n A(k/n)\binom nk w^k(1-w)^{n-k}\). They converge uniformly to \(A\), preserve endpoint values, and are convex because their second derivatives are sums of nonnegative discrete second differences of \(A(k/n)\). They also satisfy the bounds: \(A_n\le1\), and Jensen's inequality gives \(A_n(w)\ge A(w)\ge\max(w,1-w)\). Each smooth \(A_n\) therefore defines a max-stable copula by the preceding calculation. Its pointwise limit is the displayed \(C\), and rectangle inequalities and margins pass to that limit. This proves the converse for every admissible Pickands function.</p></div></details>
<h2 id="block-maxima">Block maxima identity</h2>
<p>Take \(n\) independent pairs \((U_i,V_i)\), each with copula \(C\) and thus uniform coordinates. Define the coordinatewise maxima \(U^\ast=\max_{1\le i\le n}U_i\) and \(V^\ast=\max_{1\le i\le n}V_i\). The transformed maxima \(\widetilde U=(U^\ast)^n\) and \(\widetilde V=(V^\ast)^n\) are uniform because \(P((U^\ast)^n\le u)=P(U_i\le u^{1/n}\ \text{for every }i)=u\). Their joint distribution is
<p>\[P(\widetilde U\le u,\widetilde V\le v)=C(u^{1/n},v^{1/n})^n.\]</p>
<p>For an extreme-value copula this equals \(C(u,v)\) for every \(n\).
