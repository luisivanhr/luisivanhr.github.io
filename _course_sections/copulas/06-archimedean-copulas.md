<section class="intro-strip" id="generator"><h2>Generator construction</h2>
<p>An [[Archimedean copula]] turns addition on the nonnegative line into a binary operation on [0,1]. An additive generator \(\varphi\) is continuous, strictly decreasing, convex, and satisfies \(\varphi(1)=0\). Its generalized inverse is</p>
<div class="math-block definition"><span class="block-label">Definition 6.1 <span>Generalized inverse</span></span><p>\[\varphi^{[-1]}(t)=\inf\{u\in[0,1]:\varphi(u)\le t\}.\]</p><p>For a strict generator, \(\varphi(0)=\infty\), so this is the ordinary inverse. For a non-strict generator it is zero after \(\varphi(0)\).</p></div>
<div class="math-block theorem"><span class="block-label">Theorem 6.2 <span>Bivariate construction</span></span><p>If \(\varphi\) is continuous, strictly decreasing, convex, and \(\varphi(1)=0\), then \[C(u,v)=\varphi^{[-1]}(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v))\] is a bivariate copula. Conversely every bivariate Archimedean copula has this form.</p></div>
<div class="math-block proof"><span class="block-label">Proof in the smooth strict case</span><p>Put \(c=C(u,v)\), assume twice differentiability, and differentiate \(\varphi(c)=\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)\). Then \(c_u=\varphi'(u)/\varphi'(c)\), and a second differentiation gives \[c_{uv}=-\frac{\varphi''(c)\varphi'(u)\varphi'(v)}{(\varphi'(c))^3}\ge0,\] since \(\varphi''\ge0\) and \(\varphi'&lt;0\). Thus the density is nonnegative; boundary values and marginal distributions follow by substitution. The full theorem removes smoothness using rectangle increments and the generalized inverse. Multiplying \(\varphi\) by a positive constant leaves \(C\) unchanged.</p></div></section>
<h2 id="families">Families and structure</h2>
<p>The product copula uses \(\varphi(t)=-\log t\), giving \(C(u,v)=uv\). The lower Fréchet bound uses \(\varphi(t)=1-t\), giving \(C(u,v)=\max(u+v-1,0)\).</p>
<div class="math-block definition"><span class="block-label">Definition 6.3 <span>Three standard choices</span></span><p>For \(\theta>0\), [[Clayton]] is \(C_\theta(u,v)=(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1)^{-1/\theta}\), generated by \(\varphi(t)=(t^{-\theta}-1)/\theta\). For \(\theta\ge1\), [[Gumbel]] is \(C_\theta(u,v)=\exp(-((-\log u)^\theta+(-\log v)^\theta)^{1/\theta})\), generated by \(\varphi(t)=(-\log t)^\theta\). [[Frank]] has parameter \(\theta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), generator \(\varphi(t)=-\log((e^{-\theta t}-1)/(e^{-\theta}-1))\), and its limit at \(\theta=0\) is independence.</p></div>
<p>What structure does the generator impose? Every Archimedean copula is symmetric. In the strict case, associativity follows by applying \(\varphi\) twice and using addition. In the non-strict case, the same identity is interpreted with the generalized inverse and truncation. The contour figure compares independence with Clayton and Gumbel at parameter \(2\), so the effect of the generator can be read directly from the level sets.</p>

<article class="exercise" id="exercise-6-1"><header class="exercise-head"><div><strong>Exercise 6.1</strong><span>Product generator.</span></div><button class="answer-button" type="button">Show answer</button></header><div class="exercise-body"><p>Use \(\varphi(t)=-\log t\) to recover \(C(u,v)=uv\).</p></div><div class="answer-panel"><div class="answer-inner"><p>\(\varphi^{-1}(s)=e^{-s}\), so \(C=e^{-(-\log u-\log v)}=uv\).</p></div></div></article>
<article class="exercise" id="exercise-6-2"><header class="exercise-head"><div><strong>Exercise 6.2</strong><span>Associativity.</span></div><button class="answer-button" type="button">Show answer</button></header><div class="exercise-body"><p>Prove associativity for a strict generator.</p></div><div class="answer-panel"><div class="answer-inner"><p>\(\varphi(C(u,v))=\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)\). Both sides have generator value \(\varphi(u)+\varphi(v)+\varphi(w)\), hence they are equal.</p></div></div></article>
<article class="exercise" id="exercise-6-3"><header class="exercise-head"><div><strong>Exercise 6.3</strong><span>Clayton diagonal.</span></div><button class="answer-button" type="button">Show answer</button></header><div class="exercise-body"><p>Find \(C_\theta(u,u)\).</p></div><div class="answer-panel"><div class="answer-inner"><p>Substitution gives \((2u^{-\theta}-1)^{-1/\theta}\). As \(\theta\downarrow0\), its logarithm tends to \(2\log u\), so the limit is \(u^2\).</p></div></div></article>
